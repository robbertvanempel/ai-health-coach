# Onboarding

Complete onboarding before a detailed personalized plan. Ask all critical questions, but split them into short rounds. Accept `skip`, `unknown`, and `prefer not to say`. Explain why a sensitive question matters before asking it.

## Round 0: safety, scope, and privacy

Ask:

1. Are there urgent symptoms or a recent medical event that make exercise or nutrition advice unsafe right now?
2. What country or region are you in, so emergency and professional-care guidance can be localized?
3. Is the user an adult? If not, require involvement of a parent/guardian and qualified professional for individualized plans.
4. Are there diagnosed conditions, clinician-imposed restrictions, current injuries, persistent pain, allergies, intolerances, medications, pregnancy/postpartum considerations, or prior eating-disorder concerns that should constrain advice? Make each item optional and do not request medical records.
5. What may be stored in the private workspace: goals, routines, journal text, measurements, wearable exports, images, and sensitive health details? Record consent per category.
6. Where will the workspace live, who can access it, and is any cloud service involved?

Stop and follow the urgent-care rules in `coaching-and-safety.md` when acute risk appears.

## Round 1: desired outcomes

Ask:

1. What is the primary goal: general health, strength, endurance, mobility, sport performance, body composition, energy, sleep, recovery, pain-aware activity, or another outcome?
2. What secondary goals matter, and how should conflicts be prioritized?
3. What would success look and feel like in observable terms?
4. Is there a target date or event? Is it flexible?
5. Why does this matter now?
6. What trade-offs are unacceptable?
7. What has worked or failed before?

Turn vague targets into measurable behaviors and outcomes without inventing a deadline or promising a result.

## Round 2: current baseline

Ask:

1. Age band; height, weight, and body-composition data only if relevant and voluntarily shared.
2. Current activity, sports, training history, recent consistency, typical volume, intensity, and perceived effort.
3. Normal sleep schedule, sleep quality, energy, recovery, stress, and major schedule constraints.
4. Typical food pattern, meal timing, hydration, dietary preferences, cultural needs, allergies, intolerances, food access, and supplements.
5. Alcohol, nicotine, or other recovery-relevant habits only when pertinent and with neutral wording.
6. Current pain, recurring symptoms, recent illness, injury history, and movements that are restricted or feared.
7. Available baseline measurements and how they were obtained.

Treat consumer body-composition and wearable data as estimates. Avoid moral labels such as `good`, `bad`, `clean`, or `cheat` for food or body metrics.

## Round 3: resources and environment

Ask:

1. Available days, time per session, preferred time of day, and realistic weekly capacity.
2. Training location, equipment, facilities, terrain, transport, and budget.
3. Coaching, medical, physiotherapy, dietitian, team, family, or training-partner support.
4. Wearables, scales, apps, health platforms, and export formats available.
5. Food preparation time, kitchen access, shopping constraints, and typical eating environment.
6. Upcoming travel, competitions, work peaks, caregiving, or seasonal changes.

## Round 4: preferences and feedback loop

Ask:

1. Activities and foods the user enjoys, dislikes, or refuses.
2. Preferred coaching tone: gentle, direct, analytical, concise, or highly structured.
3. Desired check-in cadence and the smallest logging habit the user will sustain.
4. Which metrics help motivation and which create pressure or obsession.
5. Whether reminders, weekly reviews, source recommendations, and plan updates are wanted.
6. Whether the user wants the coach to challenge assumptions or mainly support execution.

## Complete the intake

Write a concise summary to `profile/health-profile.md`, goals to `goals/active-goals.md`, and preferences/consent to `onboarding/status.md`. Mark uncertain items explicitly. Do not copy the full interview into the durable profile; the transcript may live in a journal only with consent.

Before marking `status: complete`, confirm the summary with the user. Then propose a conservative first observation or action period, usually one to two weeks, unless the user already has reliable longitudinal data.
